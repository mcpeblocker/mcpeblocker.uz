## Passage
- I once let a stream flow through my garden. I simply accepted the flow and let her in.
- It made my garden bloom with youth and freshness of the flowers, shine with bright sunlight reflected from the greenery formed by the stream.
- Then the stream began to get bigger and turned into a river.
Now, I thought, now I have everything I need in life - the greenery, the sunlight, the ever-flowing water.
- It was so sudden, however, when along the same channel that carried a little stream a flood found its way into my garden (turns out stream wasn’t alone, but carried else along).
- The flood could not be contained within the channel and most regretfully destroyed my garden. My shining, once alive garden had now been turned into ruins.
- So I built levees around the channel - to protect from such little streams infiltrating my garden later again.
- It worked well. Although not fully, my garden slowly recovered over time. Birds started to come back and sing in it, it began to see the sun and get alive again.
- I am still scared though.
- I now have levees to not let my garden get destroyed so easily.
- Yet if the water rises slightly (ever so slightly) above the levee, it all gets destroyed once again.
- With a much larger impact that’s been accumulating thanks to the levees.
- Those levees I trusted to be my saviors.

## Hydrology
Taking the ‘Hydrology for Engineers’ course was not a waste of time & credits after all. Not only due to the fact that I learned more than I ever did about the water life-cycle, but I also got to know little bit about protection methods against flooding.

Flooding can be defined as an event when the water level (height) within the structure rises above what the structure can hold. Say the structure we’re talking about is a little stream used for irrigation in a typical village field.

If we let X amount of water per unit of time flow within that stream we may experience flooding - because the water height that is generated by discharging X amount of water per unit of time is above the vertical height of the stream. 
If on the other hand, we let the same X amount of water per unit of time flow through the canal in a major basin - it may merely feel like leftovers of the rain.
Notice why the flooding is context-dependent - whether it floods or not is dependent on the structure you are relying on. This is the advantage for us - we can engineer our structures in very smart ways to accommodate such extreme flooding threats.

![Levee illustration](/static/images/blog/those-levees-i-trusted-to-be-my-saviors/mwloy9atk6o.webp)

Levees are one such method used as a flood protection measure. The idea is to put artificial walls with much greater height on both sides of the channel so that if the water level rises above usual, the channel can still contain it. However, they are not perfect in protecting from flooding hazards (if they were, we could use linearity property and avoid all the flooding events). The problem is that if the water height within the channel rises above the height of the levee then the other side of levee (which has been assuming safety for a long time now) experiences extremely large flood at once (second image).

![Image of failed levee consequences](/static/images/blog/those-levees-i-trusted-to-be-my-saviors/mwloz3k675p.webp)

If there was no levee to begin with:
 1) the flood would have happened earlier while its impact is way less and would have been managed in its small scale,
 2) the other side of levee (the one used to being safe) would not be so much relaxed as to stay unprepared for extreme flood.

![Image taken while crossing France headed to Italy](/static/images/blog/those-levees-i-trusted-to-be-my-saviors/mwlp5aq678d.webp)

This trade-off levee presented has inspired me to try transforming the ‘emotional safety boundaries’ we build into the concept of levee protecting the garden - ‘inner peace, happiness and stability’. The passage above is the result of such inspiration put into simple words during a lengthy travel to Italy.


## The Levee
*(Poetic version of the passage above. Credits go to Claude.)*

```
Once I let a stream come winding
through my garden, never minding
where she rose, or of what kin;
asked no question, set no measure,
took her coming for a pleasure,
simply smiled, and let her in.

Soon the beds and borders flowered,
young again and freshly showered,
dew still on the petals' skin;
sunlight, on the wet leaves gleaming,
set the whole green garden streaming,
all the morning pouring in.

Then the stream, my gentle giver,
swelled and widened to a river.
"Now," I thought, "what's left to win?
Green things growing, sunlight glowing,
water ever, ever flowing:
all I'll ever need is in."

Sudden, in the night, a roaring:
down that same small channel, pouring
black on black, came wave and din.
She'd not come alone. Behind her
stalked a flood, and I, the blinder
for my joy, had let it in.

No bank held it. Bed and border,
wall and walk and all their order,
every living thing therein,
all that shone, and all that flourished,
all the stream herself had nourished:
gone. And ruin settled in.

So I built. With spade and barrow,
round that channel, slight and narrow,
raised a levee and its twin;
swore no little stream, however
sweet her voice, again would ever
wind her way, or trickle, in.

And it held. The seasons mended,
slowly, what the flood had ended.
Never all. But, to begin,
birdsong came. The year was turning;
leaf by leaf the beds were learning
how to let the sunlight in.

Still, I'm frightened. True, no shower
drowns my garden in an hour
now. But rain taps, and within
I can feel the water, heavy,
leaning, leaning on the levee,
every storm it's gathered in.

Let it rise, but slightly, slightly,
past the crest I packed so tightly,
by a ripple, paper-thin,
and the walls I trust have saved me
loose the greater flood they gave me:
all they've stored comes thundering in.
```
